# Planning, assets and UX

## Plan

Identify platform, genre, target audience, content limits, input methods, release scope and references. Record open decisions and milestones. A smaller playable slice can establish navigation, a character, a quest, a save and a complete outcome before expanding.

Create an asset inventory for each location/background, day/evening/night variation, map and unlock highlight, character expressions and poses, item icons, interface elements, cover, audio, fonts and credits. Record source, permissions, dimensions, format, transparency and intended usage. Keep source files; do not substitute third-party reference art for licensed production assets.

## Exploration and interfaces

Map clickable areas to their visual locations. Use correct hover art and small legible location names when requested; hover-only feedback needs a touch-friendly alternative. Respect night availability and do not accidentally advance time when entering a location that should not do so. Design navigation back to the main menu separately from starting a new game.

Test dialogue and choice boxes over bright and dark backgrounds, long strings, rollback, history and small displays. Align controls, notifications and currency so they do not overlap. Verify phone menus, inventory icons, settings sliders and save slots in context rather than only checking syntax.

## Maintain a checklist

Track design, story routes, characters, assets, gameplay systems, UI, audio, accessibility, testing, desktop packaging, mobile packaging, monetization, store page, privacy and publication. Mark an item complete only with appropriate evidence; record unresolved decisions, known bugs and actual device results.
